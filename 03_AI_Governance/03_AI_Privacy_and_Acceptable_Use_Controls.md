# AI PRIVACY AND ACCEPTABLE-USE CONTROLS



**Document Classification:** Internal — Governance and Compliance  

**Document Owner:** Lead GRC & Compliance Analyst  

**Version:** 1.0  

**Status:** Draft for Management Review  

**Review Frequency:** Annually or following significant regulatory or technological changes



\---



## 1. PURPOSE



This document establishes mandatory privacy, security, and acceptable-use controls for employees, contractors, consultants, and approved third parties using Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems.



Its purpose is to prevent unauthorised disclosure of personal data, confidential business information, credentials, and other sensitive organisational information through AI applications.



## 2. SCOPE



These controls apply to:



\- Public generative AI platforms.

\- Enterprise-approved AI services.

\- Internally developed machine-learning applications.

\- AI-enabled productivity and business applications.

\- Employees, contractors, consultants, and third-party service providers acting on behalf of the organisation.



## 3. APPROVED AI USE



Employees may use approved AI applications for authorised business activities, including:



\- Drafting general business communications.

\- Summarising non-confidential information.

\- Brainstorming and generating ideas.

\- Supporting software development using approved and non-sensitive inputs.

\- Preparing training and educational materials.

\- Analysing publicly available information.



AI-generated content must be reviewed for accuracy, relevance, confidentiality, and appropriateness before official use.



## 4. PROHIBITED AI USE



Employees must not:



1\. Upload customer personal data to unapproved public AI services.

2\. Submit passwords, API keys, authentication tokens, or private cryptographic keys.

3\. Upload confidential financial records or proprietary business documents without explicit authorisation.

4\. Submit sensitive personal data to an AI system without an approved lawful basis, appropriate safeguards, and authorisation.

5\. Use AI tools to bypass organisational security controls.

6\. Rely entirely on AI-generated outputs for consequential business decisions without appropriate human review.

7\. Use AI to generate deceptive, discriminatory, fraudulent, or malicious content.

8\. Use AI-generated code in production without appropriate security review and testing.



## 5. PERSONAL DATA PROTECTION



All AI-related processing of personal information must comply with applicable data protection requirements, including the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and GDPR where applicable.



Required controls include:



\- Identify the purpose and lawful basis for processing personal data.

\- Collect and process only information necessary for the approved purpose.

\- Apply data minimisation and retention limits.

\- Restrict access to authorised users.

\- Encrypt personal data where appropriate.

\- Provide required privacy information to affected individuals.

\- Assess whether a Data Protection Impact Assessment is required.

\- Establish secure deletion procedures.

\- Assess international data transfers and applicable safeguards.



## 6. EXTERNAL AI SERVICE AND DPA REQUIREMENTS



Before personal data or confidential information is processed through an external AI provider, the organisation must conduct a documented vendor assessment.



Where the provider processes personal data on the organisation's behalf, an appropriate Data Processing Agreement (DPA) must be established where legally required.



The review must consider:



\- Data ownership and permitted use.

\- Whether submitted information is retained or used for model training.

\- Data hosting locations and international transfers.

\- Encryption and access restrictions.

\- Subcontractor arrangements.

\- Security incident notification obligations.

\- Data retention and deletion commitments.

\- Audit rights and termination arrangements.



A DPA alone does not automatically make an AI service safe or legally compliant. Technical safeguards, lawful processing, and organisational approval are also required.



## 7. DATA ANONYMISATION AND MINIMISATION



Where business analysis requires the use of datasets, employees must remove unnecessary identifying information before submitting data to an AI application.



Examples include:



\- Replacing customer names with fictional identifiers.

\- Removing email addresses and telephone numbers.

\- Removing account numbers and authentication details.

\- Aggregating records where individual identification is unnecessary.

\- Using synthetic data for testing where practical.



Pseudonymised data may still constitute personal data if an individual can be re-identified. It must therefore remain protected.



## 8. AI OUTPUT VALIDATION



AI-generated information must not automatically be treated as accurate or authoritative.



Before using AI outputs, employees must:



\- Verify important facts against reliable sources.

\- Review generated recommendations for errors and bias.

\- Check generated code for security vulnerabilities.

\- Confirm that outputs do not disclose confidential information.

\- Obtain human approval for consequential decisions.

\- Avoid presenting fabricated or unverified information as established fact.



## 9. ACCESS CONTROL AND MONITORING



The organisation must:



\- Maintain an inventory of approved AI applications.

\- Restrict access according to business need.

\- Enforce strong authentication where supported.

\- Review user permissions periodically.

\- Monitor significant AI-related security events where lawful and proportionate.

\- Maintain records of approvals and relevant policy exceptions.

\- Remove access when an employee or contractor no longer requires it.



## 10. AI SECURITY INCIDENT REPORTING



Employees must promptly report suspected AI-related incidents, including:



\- Accidental disclosure of personal or confidential information.

\- Unauthorised access to AI applications.

\- Exposure of passwords, tokens, or credentials.

\- Suspicious AI-generated instructions or prompt-injection activity.

\- Unauthorised use of organisational data for model training.

\- Harmful or misleading AI outputs affecting business operations.



The incident response team must assess the incident, preserve relevant evidence, contain the exposure, and escalate it according to organisational procedures.



Where a personal data breach occurs, the organisation must assess its notification obligations and applicable regulatory deadlines.



## 11. POLICY EXCEPTIONS



Exceptions to this policy must:



\- Be submitted in writing.

\- Identify the business justification.

\- Include a privacy and security risk assessment.

\- Specify the data and AI application involved.

\- Include compensating safeguards.

\- Receive approval from authorised management and relevant privacy or security representatives.



No employee may independently approve an exception involving prohibited data disclosure.



## 12. TRAINING AND AWARENESS



Employees and contractors must receive appropriate AI privacy and acceptable-use training.



Training must address:



\- Safe use of generative AI.

\- Personal data protection.

\- Confidential information


- Secure handling of customer, employee and vendor information.
- Verification of AI-generated information before use.
- Prompt injection and other AI-related security risks.
- Reporting suspected AI misuse, privacy breaches or security incidents.
- Human oversight when using AI systems and autonomous agents.

Training must be completed during onboarding and refreshed periodically. Completion records must be maintained.

## 13. AUTONOMOUS AI AGENT CONTROLS

Autonomous AI agents must operate within formally approved business purposes and documented boundaries.

The following safeguards are mandatory:

- Apply least-privilege access to systems, files, APIs and business applications.
- Restrict agents to approved tools, data sources and authorised actions.
- Require human approval before high-impact, external or irreversible actions.
- Prevent agents from independently approving payments, changing access privileges or disclosing confidential information.
- Validate agent instructions and outputs before execution where security or business impact is material.
- Maintain logs of agent instructions, tool calls, actions, approvals and exceptions.
- Provide a mechanism to suspend or disable an agent when unsafe behaviour is detected.
- Review agent permissions and activity periodically.

An autonomous agent must not be granted broader authority than is necessary for its approved purpose.

## 14. COMPLIANCE MONITORING AND ENFORCEMENT

The organisation must periodically assess compliance with this policy.

Monitoring activities may include:

- Reviewing approved AI application inventories.
- Checking access permissions and usage logs.
- Reviewing data-sharing and third-party AI arrangements.
- Assessing compliance with privacy and security requirements.
- Investigating reported misuse and policy violations.
- Tracking corrective actions to closure.

Policy violations must be reported through the organisation's established management, privacy or information security channels.

Where a violation creates a privacy or security incident, the organisation's incident response and breach management procedures must be followed.

## 15. POLICY REVIEW AND APPROVAL

This policy must be reviewed at least annually and whenever significant changes occur in AI technology, applicable laws, business operations or the organisation's risk environment.

Updates must be approved by authorised management and the relevant privacy, legal and information security representatives.
